How one photo becomes a whole 3D scene

Pretrained models do the perception and the generation. The geometry that connects them — the camera model, the meshing, the plane fitting, the placement solver — is this project's own code.

Tier 1 — whole-scene mesh

1
Depth estimation

Depth Anything V2 predicts a depth value for every pixel of the photo.

2
Unprojection

Each pixel plus its depth is turned into a 3D point through a pinhole camera model. Focal length comes from EXIF when the photo carries it, and from an assumed 60° field of view when it doesn't.

3
Edge-aware triangulation

Neighbouring pixels are joined into triangles — except where they straddle a depth jump, or where the resulting triangle would be nearly edge-on to the camera. Without those two tests, every object gets welded to the wall behind it by a sheet of stretched "skin". Toggle Wireframe to see the result.

4
Texturing

The photo itself becomes the texture: every vertex knows which pixel it came from, so no colour is resampled or invented.

Tier 2 — compositional scene

5
Instance segmentation

SAM2 proposes masks; custom filtering reduces ~100 raw masks to the handful that are actually distinct foreground objects, discarding structure, duplicates and sub-parts.

6
Room shell

Every pixel not claimed by an object is back-projected, and planes are fitted to that cloud with RANSAC to recover the floor, walls and ceiling. Candidate inliers must match the plane in surface orientation, not just distance — otherwise a tilted plane grazing a wall collects thousands of wall pixels and passes as a floor.

7
Per-object generation

Each object is cropped out using its own mask and passed through TripoSR, which produces a complete mesh — including the parts the camera never saw.

8
Placement

Each generated mesh arrives in an arbitrary canonical pose. Solving for the scale, position and rotation that make it match where the object really sits — measured against its own back-projected points — is the core of the project.

What single-image reconstruction cannot do

Tier 1 only ever reconstructs visible surfaces. The back of every object, anything behind anything else, and anything outside the frame is simply absent. That is a property of the problem, not a gap in the implementation — the information is not in the photograph.

Tier 2 fills those gaps by inventing them. TripoSR generates an object's unseen sides from what it learned across ~800,000 3D models. Plausible, and often convincing — but a guess, not a measurement.

Scale is ambiguous. Without known camera intrinsics, a small object nearby and a large one far away produce the same photo. The pipeline resolves this by holding one estimated focal length constant across the whole scene, which keeps objects consistent relative to each other without claiming true-to-life measurements.
